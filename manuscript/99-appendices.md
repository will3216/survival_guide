## Appendix B — The Numbers, On One Page

I have put the figures together here because that is what people ask for, and I want it on the page that what came off them to make them fit was the warnings, and the warnings were the point. A table is light enough to carry and light enough to copy out, and a copy of a copy is a figure with nothing holding it down. Each one has its chapter on it. Go and read the chapter.

### Table 1 — Boiling. Chapter 4.

| Where you are | Hold it at a rolling boil |
|---|---|
| Ordinary ground | one minute |
| Above about two thousand metres | three minutes |
| You do not know how high you are | three minutes |

A rolling boil is moving and breaking the surface, not steaming. Cover it while it cools.

### Table 2 — Bleach. Chapter 4.

| Water | Dose per four litres | Per litre | Stand it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clear | an eighth of a teaspoon | about two drops | thirty minutes |
| Still cloudy after straining and settling | a quarter teaspoon | about four drops | thirty minutes |
| Cold, below about ten degrees | as above | as above | one hour |
| Near freezing | as above | as above | two hours |

Plain unscented household bleach, sodium hypochlorite at five to nine per cent and nothing else in it. Doubling is never a way round settling, and the ordinary dose is a floor. **And the caveat that goes everywhere this table goes: a parasite common in surface water is not reliably killed by chlorine at any dose you would willingly drink.** Boiling kills it. So does a bought filter rated a micron absolute or finer.

### Table 3 — Stopping blood, in order. Chapter 3.

| | Direct pressure | Packing | Tourniquet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where | anywhere it bleeds | a hole in an arm, a leg, or where one joins the body | an arm or a leg only |
| How | heel of the hand on one exact spot, second hand stacked, arms locked, your weight | cloth fed in until full and tight, then both hands on top | wide strap high and tight between wound and body, never over a joint, tightened until bleeding stops |
| Hold | ten minutes at least | three minutes at least | secure the windlass, write the time on |
| Never | lift off to look | pack a chest, belly, head, eye or neck | loosen it yourself |

A neck is pressure on one side only, never both, and nothing wrapped round it. An open chest wound is covered with cloth, never plastic, and never packed.

### Table 4 — Oral rehydration, one litre. Chapter 12.

| Clean water | Sugar | Salt |
|---|---|---|
| one litre | six level teaspoons | half a level teaspoon |

Err under on the salt: no saltier than tears. The water has to be clean — boiled or treated — and that outranks the recipe. A sealed packet of the proper salts beats any kitchen version and is used exactly as printed.

### Table 5 — Calories a day. Chapter 6.

| The day you are having | Roughly |
|---|---|
| Lying still and warm | two thousand to two and a half thousand |
| Digging, hauling, walking all day | three thousand to four and a half thousand |
| Hard work in cold weather under a load | measured higher again |

### Table 6 — Where the latrine goes. Chapter 12.

| | |
|---|---|
| From any well, spring, stream or standing water anybody drinks | thirty metres at least |
| Pit bottom above where water stands in the ground | a metre and a half at least |
| From the beds | no more than about fifty metres, downwind, off the water path |
| A day or two | a hole a hand's depth, filled in after |
| Weeks | a trench a hand's span wide, as deep as your arm is long |
| Staying | a pit two to three metres down, boarded, with a lid, closed half a metre from the top |

Thirty is written for ground somebody surveyed. I pace fifty.

### Table 7 — How long fuel lasts. Chapters 10 and 8.

| Fuel | Sealed, cool |
|---|---|
| Gasoline | roughly three to six months; older is a maybe, not a fuel |
| Diesel | something like a year if clean and cool |
| Lamp oil | keeps best of the three |

Petrol goes gummy in months rather than years even sealed. Diesel grows things in the water at the bottom of the drum.
