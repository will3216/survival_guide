# The Long After — Continuity Bible

Reference document. Lives outside `manuscript/`, is never linted. Read this
before naming anything in a chapter — it is the single source of truth for
who is alive, who is dead, what year it is, and which Rule number means what.

Every name recorded here is a name that will appear in `manuscript/`, so
nothing here may collide with `notes/forbidden-terms.txt`. Checked on write.

---

## Narrator

Unnamed throughout the book. People call him by role (the apprentice, the
hand, the old man) or by whatever the current group calls him — never by a
fixed name, and the manuscript never supplies one. Roughly sixty when he
writes this. He was an adult, barely, when the Collapse happened. He has
belonged to eleven groups over the years; six are named in the book.

He has worn glasses since he was nine. The left lens cracked on the first
morning of Y0, during the hour he spent patting a floor for them instead of
filling a bath (chapter 01), and he is wearing those same glasses at Y40, the
year he writes the book. The crack has never been repaired and the glasses
have never been replaced. There is no duration to interpret here: assume the
cracked left lens at any point from Y0 to Y40, including while he writes.
Nobody is making lenses any more and he knows it. This is permanent canon
from chapter 01 and is available to every later chapter —
chapters 08 (gear), 09 (scavenging), 12 (slow deaths) and 17 especially.
Do not give him good eyesight and do not quietly replace the glasses.

## Debts owed by later chapters

Obligations the front matter (`manuscript/00-front-matter.md`) created for
chapters not yet written. Check this section before finishing the chapter
named.

- **Chapter 3 owes the long version of direct pressure.** Chapter 01 puts
  "Am I bleeding?" ahead of airway on purpose, gives only the minimum
  technique (look as well as feel; press hard, directly, with whatever is
  already in your hand; do not keep lifting off to check) and says in text
  that "Chapter 3 is the long version and you should read it before you need
  it." Chapter 03 ("The Body Is Your First Vehicle") must therefore carry the
  full treatment of controlling serious external bleeding — direct pressure
  done properly, packing, and whatever it chooses to say about tourniquets —
  or chapter 01's promise to the reader goes unpaid. Chapter 02 also points a
  go-bag line at it ("a small roll of what you use to stop bleeding").

- **Chapter 13 owes four lines about a refrigerator.** The editor's
  preface says the narrator gives four lines, in his chapter on keeping
  food, to "a thing called a refrigerator: a box that was cold inside, all
  the time, by itself, in every house" — and that the editor has read
  those four lines more often than anything else ever written. Chapter 13
  is "Growing Things, Keeping Things." It must contain four lines
  describing a refrigerator that read, to someone two generations later,
  like the kind of thing worth re-reading with longing — not a throwaway
  mention. The preface has already promised the reader this passage
  exists.

## Available canon for later chapters

Facts the front matter established that later chapters may draw on
directly. Use them; do not reinvent or contradict them.

- **The three questions are a book-level device, and their wording is
  fixed.** Chapter 01 establishes them, asked in this order, as the spine
  of the whole book. The exact wording is:

  1. **Am I bleeding?**
  2. **Can I breathe where I am standing?**
  3. **Does anyone know where I am?**

  Any later chapter that re-invokes them — in prose, in a Short Version
  bullet, or in an illustration caption — **must reuse this wording
  verbatim and must keep this order**. Do not paraphrase, do not compress,
  do not modernise ("is anyone looking for me", "can I breathe here",
  "am I hurt" are all wrong). Chapter 02 re-invokes all three, in order and
  with the wording above exactly, in one place: the last paragraph of its
  `## Which Way Out` section, immediately before stepping out of the door.
  It also quotes the third question alone twice — in its opening hand-off
  from chapter 01, and once inside `## Staying Still Is Also a Decision` —
  again with the wording unchanged. It does **not** put any of them in its
  Short Version. That is the pattern to copy: full set at the moment of
  acting, the single question wherever that question alone is the one
  deciding something, question marks kept in every case. Re-invoking them
  is optional; changing them is not permitted.

- **Chapter 02 is the chapter chapter 01 named.** Chapter 01's third
  question, *does anyone know where I am?*, carries the in-text line "This
  question decides whether you move, which is the whole of the next
  chapter." That cross-reference points at chapter 02 ("Run or Dig In"),
  which answers it: the shelter-in-place versus evacuate decision, the
  trap of leaving late, the go-bag, and route choice. Chapter 02 picks the
  hand-off up explicitly in its opening. Recorded here so chapter 01's
  forward reference is traceable and so no later chapter claims the same
  hand-off.

- **The Spoke's evacuation discipline (chapter 02).** The Spoke kept a bag
  room with eleven packed bags in it, weighed, labelled and re-checked
  monthly, and walked the whole group out of the house and back in twice a
  year as a drill. Their rule for the stay-or-go decision was Halla Dorn's
  sentence: decide by noon, say it out loud, and move while the light is
  still on your side. They were boring, unglamorous and alive; a house of
  cleverer people down the same valley deliberated for three days and were
  not. Available to any chapter that wants the Spoke's competence as a
  measuring stick — the Spoke's whole character is that preparation looks
  stupid right up until it does not.

- **The Rules are folk-quoted, and quoted wrong.** The editor's preface
  states the Rules are repeated daily, in the yard and over the fire, by
  people who have never read the book and would not read it if handed it
  — and that they get them wrong, and the editor has stopped correcting
  them. This is available canon for **chapter 15** (How Groups Decide
  Things) and **chapter 19** (Holidays, Songs, and Other Load-Bearing
  Nonsense) — the Rules having outgrown the book and become folklore is a
  gift to both a chapter about group governance and a chapter about
  ritual and oral transmission.

## Rule ledger

A Markdown table with columns `| # | Rule | Chapter |`. **Every row must
begin with the number so `scripts/lint_manuscript.py`'s `LEDGER_ROW_RE` can
read it** — do not reformat this table, do not indent it, do not reorder the
columns.

Add a row here in the **same commit** as the chapter that first cites the
Rule. `check_rules_cited` fails the build on any `Rule N` mentioned in a
chapter whose number is not a row below.

|---|------|---------|

| 9 | Water first. Always. | 04 |
| 11 | The second floor is not safer. It is only harder to leave. | 02 |
| 23 | Pack the bag for the stairs, not for the road. | 02 |
| 80 | *Reserved. Spent in chapter 20. Do not cite before then.* | 20 |

**Rule 80 is reserved.** It is the final rule — the soft rule that outranks
the hard ones. Its number and chapter are seeded here now so the ledger is
complete, but its text is deliberately not written in this task. It is
written only in chapter 20. **No chapter before chapter 20 may cite Rule
80.** The linter cannot enforce this — `check_rules_cited` would happily
pass an early citation — so this is an editorial rule, not a mechanical one.
Treat it as load-bearing anyway.
